Google's own 2026 GSoC announcement flagged AI as reshaping open source communities, and many orgs now publish AI-use policies. Maintainers have seen a flood of generated pull requests. Sustained, clearly human engagement is what separates you from that flood.

## Read the policy first

Look for an AI-use policy in the contributor guide, the code of conduct, or the GSoC page. Policies range from “disclose any AI assistance” to “no AI-generated code”. Follow your org's exactly. If there's none, ask in chat; asking shows good judgement.

## Good uses

- Explaining unfamiliar code, error messages or build systems while you read them.
- Suggesting where in a large codebase something might live, which you then verify.
- Drafting tests you then read, run and fix.
- Checking your own writing for clarity, keeping your voice and your ideas.

## Uses that backfire

- **Submitting code you can't explain.** In review, you'll be asked why. If you can't answer, trust is gone.
- **Generated issue comments and chat messages.** They're easy to spot and read as low effort.
- **A generated proposal.** Mentors read dozens; generic plans with no real file names or trade-offs stand out immediately.
- **Mass-produced pull requests** across many issues. This is the pattern that made orgs write AI policies.

## A simple rule

Before you submit anything, ask: “Could I explain every line of this to a maintainer on a call, and defend why it's written this way?” If not, it's not ready.
