After two or three merged PRs, the most valuable thing you can do is focus. Depth in one community beats shallow contributions across five.

## Pick your primary org

By mid-December, choose one org to focus on (keep a backup if you like). Choose the one where:

- your PRs got thoughtful, timely reviews,
- you enjoyed the codebase, and
- the ideas you'd want to work on match your skills.

## Take on a medium issue

Look for work a step up from your first PRs: a small feature, a bug that crosses two modules, a performance problem with a benchmark. Ask a maintainer “I've done #12 and #34; is there something meatier in <area> you'd like help with?” Maintainers often have tasks they'd hand to a trusted newcomer.

## Review other people's pull requests

Reviewing is one of the most valued and least crowded ways to contribute. Start small:

- Check out a PR, run it, and confirm whether it fixes the issue.
- Point out missing tests or edge cases, kindly.
- Answer newcomers' questions in chat with what you learned last month.

It teaches you the codebase faster than anything else, and maintainers notice who's helping carry the load.

## Pitch an idea in public

The strongest program proposals grow out of issues the applicant already worked on. Mentors often fold good community proposals into the official ideas list, and the person who started the thread has a natural edge.

Open a discussion issue in December or January with:

1. **The problem**: what's slow, missing or painful today, with evidence (an issue, a benchmark, user reports).
2. **A rough design**: the approach, the parts of the code it touches, alternatives you considered.
3. **A small proof of concept**: a draft PR showing the approach works on a slice of the problem.
4. **Questions**: what you're unsure about.

Unglamorous, clearly scoped ideas close to the core codebase are the safest bets: observability, performance, API modernisation, data import and export, tests and tooling.

## Become a familiar name

None of this needs to be loud. Show up regularly: a PR or review most weeks, helpful answers in chat, summaries of decisions. By February, when the org list is announced, you want the mentors to read your proposal and think “oh good, it's them”.
