Getting selected is the start. Here's how to make the program go well for you and your mentors.

## Community bonding

The weeks between results and coding are for:

- Meeting your mentors (a call if possible) and agreeing how you'll communicate: where, how often, and how quickly to expect replies.
- Turning your proposal into a concrete plan: issues or a project board for each milestone.
- Landing a few small PRs near the code you'll change, to get the review loop going.
- Setting up anything slow: access, test data, CI permissions.

## Weekly rhythm

- **A short weekly update** in a public place (the issue, a discussion thread, a blog if the org asks): what you did, what's next, what's blocking you.
- **Small, reviewable PRs** rather than one large one. Review is the bottleneck; feed it steadily.
- **Raise problems the day you find them.** “This is taking longer than planned because X; options are A or B” is a normal, welcome message. Silence is the thing mentors can't work with.

## Evaluations

Programs check in at the midpoint and the end. Mentors evaluate you, and you evaluate them. You pass by doing what you said you'd do, or by having clearly agreed changes to the plan. Don't let an evaluation be the first time your mentor hears about a problem.

## When things go sideways

- **Scope is bigger than expected**: agree with your mentor which stretch goals to drop. Do it early.
- **You're stuck for more than a day or two**: ask in public with what you tried.
- **Life happens** (illness, exams moved): tell your mentor and org admin as soon as you know. Programs have processes for extensions and pauses.
- **Mentor is unresponsive**: ping the org admin; that's part of their job.

## Finish strong

- Merge your work; unmerged code helps no one.
- Write a final report that says what was done, what wasn't and why, and how to pick up where you left off. Link every PR.
- Thank your mentors publicly.

## Stay on

The best outcome isn't the certificate, it's becoming part of the project. Keep reviewing, help next year's applicants, and consider mentoring yourself.
