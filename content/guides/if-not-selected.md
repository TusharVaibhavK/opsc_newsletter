With acceptance rates around 7.5% for GSoC, most applicants aren't selected, including many who did everything right. Slots are limited, and orgs often have more strong applicants than they can fund.

## The first week

- Take a day off from it. Then remember what you have that you didn't have in October: merged code, reviews, a codebase you understand, and maintainers who know your name.
- **Ask for feedback once, politely, in public or by the org's stated channel:** “I wasn't selected this year and I'd like to keep contributing. Is there anything in my proposal or contributions you'd suggest I improve?” Not everyone has time to reply; don't push.

## Keep contributing

The contributors mentors remember are the ones who stayed after results. Many people are selected on their second application with the same org, because by then they're already doing the work.

## Apply on another calendar

Programs run on different schedules, so the next chance is usually months away, not a year:

- **LFX Mentorship** runs three terms a year (Mar–May, Jun–Aug, Sep–Nov).
- **Outreachy** has May and December cohorts, if you're eligible.
- **European Summer of Code**, **C4GT**, **FOSSEE**, **Summer of Bitcoin** and others run on their own timelines.

The [timeline](../../timeline/) shows what's open next, and the [program finder](../../finder/) filters by eligibility.

## Keep the work visible

Write a short post about what you built and learned. Pin your best PRs on your GitHub profile. It's useful for the next application, and for jobs.
